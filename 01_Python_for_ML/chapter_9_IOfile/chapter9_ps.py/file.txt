Once upon a time, a salt seller lived in a small village. He owned a gadha, which he used to carry heavy sacks of salt to the nearby market every day. 
The merchant and his gadha had to cross a river on their way to the market. 
One day, while crossing the river, the gadha slipped and fell into the water. 
The salt in the sacks dissolved, making the load much lighter. The gadha was very happy and felt relieved. 
The next day, the lazy gadha intentionally slipped into the river again to lighten his load, and the merchant suffered a huge loss because nobody bought the wet salt. 
The merchant realized the gadha was playing tricks. 
To teach the lazy gadha a lesson, the merchant loaded the gadha with cotton instead of salt on the third day. 
When the gadha fell into the river on purpose, the cotton soaked up the water and became multiple times heavier. 
The gadha struggled to carry the heavy load to the market, learning a tough lesson. 
From that day on, the gadha never tried to trick his master again.
